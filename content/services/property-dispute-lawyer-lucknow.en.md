Property disputes in Lucknow are among the most common civil matters, ranging from ancestral partition disagreements to illegal possession and boundary disputes. These matters are decided primarily on documentary evidence — title deeds, revenue records, and proof of possession — making early and organised preparation important.

## Who Needs This Page

This page is relevant if you are involved in a dispute over property ownership, possession, or boundaries in Lucknow — whether you are trying to protect your title and possession, seeking partition of jointly-held property, or responding to a claim brought against you.

## Common Lucknow Dispute Types

- **Ancestral property partition**, where co-owners — often siblings or extended family — disagree on how a jointly inherited property should be divided
- **Illegal possession and encroachment**, where a party occupies land or a portion of it without legal right
- **Sale-deed fraud**, involving forged or fraudulently executed documents purporting to transfer title
- **Boundary disputes**, over the exact limits of adjoining properties
- **Landlord-tenant disagreements**, which follow a related but distinct procedure under tenancy law

## Remedies Available

Depending on the nature of the dispute, a civil suit may seek one or more of the following:

- **Declaration of title** — a court declaration confirming ownership where it is disputed
- **Possession suit** — recovery of possession where a party has been wrongfully dispossessed
- **Temporary and permanent injunction** — an order restraining construction, dispossession, or interference with possession while the dispute is pending (temporary) or as a final remedy (permanent)
- **Partition suit** — division of jointly-owned property into individual shares
- **Recovery of mesne profits** — compensation for the fair rental value of property during a period of unlawful occupation

## How Injunctions Work in Practice

A temporary injunction is often the most urgent relief sought in property disputes, particularly to halt illegal construction or prevent dispossession while the underlying suit is pending. The court examines whether there is a prima facie case in the applicant's favour, whether the balance of convenience supports granting the order, and whether irreparable harm would result without it. In genuinely urgent situations, courts can pass an ex-parte ad-interim order — without first hearing the other side — on the date of filing itself, followed by a full hearing on notice to the opposite party.

## Partition Suit Stages

A partition suit generally proceeds through two decree stages:

1. **Preliminary decree**, where the court determines the shares each co-owner is entitled to, based on title and family relationships.
2. **Final decree**, where the property is actually divided — physically or through sale and distribution of proceeds — in accordance with the shares determined earlier.

This two-stage process means partition litigation can take considerable time where the property or the family structure is complex, particularly if the preliminary decree itself is contested.

## Evidence That Matters

Property disputes are decided heavily on documentary evidence. The most relevant documents typically include:

- Registered sale deeds and prior title documents in the chain of ownership
- Khatauni and other revenue records showing recorded ownership or tenancy
- Mutation entries reflecting changes in recorded ownership
- Property tax receipts and utility records showing possession and use
- Photographs, witness statements, or other proof of physical possession

## Limitation Periods Apply — Act Early

Property disputes are subject to limitation periods under the Limitation Act, 1963, meaning a claim can become time-barred if not brought within the prescribed period. The specific limitation period depends on the nature of the claim — possession, declaration of title, or recovery. Because of this, it is advisable to consult early rather than delay once a dispute becomes apparent.

## Civil vs Criminal Routes in Possession Disputes

Where a property has been taken over through fraud, forgery, or force, the aggrieved party often has both a civil and a criminal option available. The civil route — a suit for declaration of title, possession, or injunction — focuses on restoring the party's legal rights over the property. Where the facts also disclose a cognizable offence, such as criminal trespass, forgery of sale documents, or intimidation, a parallel criminal complaint or FIR may be pursued at a police station having jurisdiction. Which combination of remedies to pursue, and in what order, depends on the specific facts, the strength of documentary evidence, and how urgently possession needs to be protected.

## Recovery Suits and Money Disputes Linked to Property

Property disputes sometimes overlap with recovery matters — for instance, where an advance was paid under an agreement to sell that was never completed, or where rent or dues remain unpaid. These recovery claims are pursued through a civil suit, generally before the District Court Lucknow, and are subject to their own limitation period, distinct from the limitation applicable to possession or title claims.

## Jurisdiction Within Lucknow

Depending on the valuation of the suit, property matters in Lucknow are heard before the Court of Civil Judge (Junior Division) for suits valued up to a threshold set by law, or the Court of Civil Judge (Senior Division) for higher-value matters, with appeals lying to the High Court of Judicature at Allahabad, Lucknow Bench. Areas such as Madiyaon, Aliganj, Gomti Nagar, Hazratganj, and Indira Nagar all fall within the jurisdiction of the District Court Lucknow for these purposes.
