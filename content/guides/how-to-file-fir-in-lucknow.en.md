Filing an FIR is often the first and most important step in getting a criminal matter formally investigated. Knowing exactly what to expect at the police station — and what to do if things don't go as they should — makes a real difference in how smoothly the process goes.

## Who Needs This Guide

This guide is for anyone in Lucknow who needs to report a crime to the police, whether as a victim or a witness, and wants to understand the correct procedure, their rights during the process, and what options exist if a station refuses to cooperate.

## What an FIR Actually Is

An FIR, or First Information Report, is the document prepared by the police under Section 173 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) — the provision that replaced Section 154 of the CrPC from 1 July 2024 — when information about a cognizable offence is given to them. Registering an FIR sets the police's investigative machinery in motion: they can arrest without a warrant, search premises, and take other investigative steps that would otherwise require separate court permission.

## Cognizable vs Non-Cognizable Offences

Not every complaint results in an FIR in the same way:

- **Cognizable offences** — generally the more serious ones, such as theft, assault causing grievous hurt, or cheating of a significant amount — are recorded directly as an FIR, and the police can begin investigating without needing a Magistrate's prior approval.
- **Non-cognizable offences** — generally less serious matters — are instead recorded as a Non-Cognizable Report (NCR). For these, the police cannot investigate on their own; the complainant typically needs to approach the Magistrate, who can direct an investigation if appropriate.

Whether a given set of facts amounts to a cognizable or non-cognizable offence depends on the specific provision of law that applies, and this classification is often the first thing worth clarifying when deciding how to proceed.

## Step-by-Step: Filing an FIR at the Station

1. **Approach the police station** having jurisdiction over the area where the offence occurred, and ask to speak to the Station House Officer (SHO) or the duty officer.
2. **Give your information**, orally or in writing. If given orally, the officer is required to reduce it to writing.
3. **Listen to it being read back to you.** Before you sign, the information as recorded must be read out to you so you can confirm it accurately reflects what you said.
4. **Sign the FIR** once you are satisfied it is correctly recorded.
5. **Collect your free copy.** You have a legal right to receive a copy of the FIR immediately and free of cost — no police officer can lawfully ask for payment for this.

## Zero FIR — Filing at Any Police Station

If the offence occurred outside the jurisdiction of the station you approach, you are not required to travel to the correct station first. Under the Zero FIR mechanism, any police station is obligated to register the FIR — assigning it a "zero" number — and then transfer it to the station with actual territorial jurisdiction, which continues the investigation under a regular FIR number. This is particularly useful in emergencies, or when the correct station is far away or the matter is urgent.

## e-FIR via UPCOP and uppolice.gov.in

Uttar Pradesh Police provides facilities to report certain categories of offences online, through the UPCOP mobile application and the uppolice.gov.in portal. In general terms, e-FIR filing tends to be available for specific categories of offences (commonly matters like vehicle theft or lost property, where investigation does not depend heavily on the immediate scene), and typically requires some form of verification before the report is treated as a formal FIR. For serious or urgent cognizable offences, or where the facts are less straightforward, approaching the police station directly and in person generally remains the more reliable route. It is worth checking the current scope and verification requirements on the official portal before relying on the online route for a specific matter.

## If the Police Refuse to Register Your FIR

Refusal to register an FIR for a cognizable offence is not lawful, and there is a clear escalation path if this happens:

1. **Written complaint to the Superintendent of Police (SP)** — under Section 173(4) BNSS, you can send the substance of the information in writing, by post, to the SP or an officer of equivalent rank. If satisfied that the information discloses a cognizable offence, the SP can either investigate the matter personally or direct that it be investigated by a subordinate officer, and can direct that the FIR be registered.
2. **Complaint before the Magistrate** — under Section 175(3) BNSS, if the police still do not act, a complaint can be made directly to the Magistrate having jurisdiction, who can direct the police to register an FIR and investigate the matter.

Keeping a written record of your visit to the station — including the date, the officer you spoke to, and what you were told — is useful if escalation becomes necessary.

## What to Include in Your FIR

A clear and complete FIR should generally cover:

- The date, time, and place of the incident
- A factual, chronological account of what happened
- The names and descriptions of persons involved, to the extent known
- Details of any witnesses
- Any property lost, damaged, or stolen, described specifically

Avoid speculation or exaggeration — the FIR is a foundational document in the case, and inconsistencies between it and later statements can be used to question credibility at trial.

## Provisions for Women Complainants

For certain offences against women — including sexual offences and related categories specified by law — the statement is required, as far as practicable, to be recorded by a woman police officer. In some situations, the law also allows the presence of a person of the complainant's choice, such as a relative or friend, while the statement is recorded, and permits recording the statement at the complainant's residence rather than at the station where circumstances warrant it.
